How it works

Capture, basket, shop

The same three steps the app is built around — nothing to set up beyond choosing your usual supermarket.

1

Capture the recipe

Paste a link or the recipe text, pick a photo or take one of a cookbook page, or send a video link. You can also share a page straight from another app into The Fern Basket. It comes back as a structured ingredient list you can check and edit.

2

Build the basket

Add as many recipes as you're cooking. Ingredients that repeat are merged into a single line, quantities scale with the number of serves, and you can add anything else you need by hand.

3

Shop it

Shop mode sorts the list by aisle and works entirely on your phone, so a supermarket basement with no signal doesn't stop you. Tick the last item and you're done.

Honest about pricing

Prices are estimates

No New Zealand supermarket offers a public price feed, so the cost comparison is an estimate to help you choose where to shop — not a quote. What you pay at the shelf can differ.

The Fern Basket will always tell you when a number is an estimate rather than a price we can stand behind.

Privacy

Deliberately boring about your data

What we don't collect

No analytics, no advertising identifiers, no contacts, no device identifiers, and no background or continuous location.

Two permissions, asked in context

Location and photo library / camera — and only at the moment you use the feature that needs them. Decline either and the rest of the app still works: type a suburb instead of using GPS, and add recipes by link or text instead of by photo.

Some things never leave your phone

Dietary and allergy tags stay on the device even when you're signed in, because they can reveal a religion or a medical condition.

The shopping half works offline

Browsing your library, building and editing baskets, Shop mode and sharing a list are all local to your phone by design.
