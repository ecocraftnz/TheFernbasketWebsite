Turn any recipe into one shopping list

The Fern Basket is a grocery app for New Zealand. Paste a recipe link, photograph a cookbook page, or share a cooking video, and it reads the ingredients into a structured list you can check and edit. Add everything you are cooking this week and the repeats collapse into single lines, so three recipes calling for butter become one entry rather than three. The finished grocery list is sorted by supermarket aisle and works on your phone without a signal, because the part of the shop that matters happens in a building with famously bad reception.

What the app does

Add a recipe from a link, a photo, or a video

Paste a URL or the recipe text, take or choose a photo of a cookbook page, or send a video link. You can also share a page straight from another app into The Fern Basket.

Duplicate ingredients merge into one line

Cook several dishes at once and shared ingredients combine automatically. Quantities scale when you change how many people you are cooking for.

A shopping list sorted by supermarket aisle

Shop mode groups the list the way the store is laid out, so you walk the aisles once. Tick items off as you go.

Works offline in the supermarket

Your library, your baskets and the shopping list itself all live on the phone. No signal in the chiller aisle does not stop you.

Compare Pak'nSave, New World and Woolworths

Find the cheapest store for your whole basket, and your nearest branches by location or suburb. Totals are estimates; shelf prices may differ.

Adapt a recipe to how you eat

Set dietary preferences once. Premium can rewrite a recipe as vegetarian, vegan or gluten-free, or produce a healthier or lower-calorie version, showing what changed and why.

From recipe to shopping list, screen by screen

Four screens from the app, following one shop from recipe to grocery list. The recipes, the basket and the store totals below are example data, not a real shop — and the prices the app shows are always estimates, because no New Zealand supermarket publishes a public price feed.

Questions people ask

Which New Zealand supermarkets does it compare?

Pak'nSave, New World and Woolworths. You can set a default store during setup and change it any time, and the app can find nearby branches using your location or a suburb you type in.

Are the supermarket prices accurate?

They are estimates, not quotes. No New Zealand supermarket publishes a public price feed, so the comparison is there to help you choose where to shop rather than to predict your receipt. What you pay at the shelf can differ, and the app says so wherever a number appears.

Can I add a recipe from a YouTube video?

Yes. Send a video link and the app reads the recipe out of it. Links to recipe pages, plain pasted text, and photos of cookbook pages or recipe cards all work too.

Does the shopping list work without internet?

Yes. Browsing your saved recipes, building and editing baskets, shop mode and sharing a list are all local to your phone by design. A connection is needed to add a new recipe, compare prices or find stores.

Can it adapt recipes for vegetarian, vegan or gluten-free diets?

Yes, as part of Premium. The app swaps ingredients one at a time so you can accept or skip each change, and it tells you honestly when a dish changes so much that it is really a different meal. You can also record preferences such as dairy-free, nut-free, halal, kosher, low-FODMAP, keto and others.

Is The Fern Basket free?

Capturing recipes, building baskets, the aisle-sorted shopping list and the store price comparison are part of the app. Premium adds the recipe rewriting — healthier, lower calorie, and dietary adaptations — and starts with a seven-day free trial on a monthly or annual plan. You can cancel any time in Settings. Prices are shown in the app in your own currency.

Do I need an account?

No. The app works without signing in. Signing in with your email adds syncing of your recipes, baskets and settings across your devices, and uses a one-time code rather than a password.

What data does the app collect?

No analytics, no advertising identifiers, no contacts, no device identifiers and no background location. It asks for exactly two permissions — location, and photo library or camera — and only at the moment you use the feature needing them. Dietary and allergy tags stay on your device even when you are signed in. The full privacy policy sets out the detail.

Is it available outside New Zealand?

Not yet. The store comparison and nearby-store lookup are built around New Zealand supermarkets, so the app is New Zealand only for now.
